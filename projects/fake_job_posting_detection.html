Fake Job Posting Detection with NLP

Natural Language Processing & Classification Pipeline Detecting Fraudulent Employment Listings

Project Overview

Developed during my Machine Learning Internship at DecodeLabs, this project tackles employment recruitment fraud by building an end-to-end Natural Language Processing (NLP) classification pipeline. Online employment scams exploit job seekers for financial extortion or identity theft, demanding automated screening mechanisms for job boards and recruitment portals.

The pipeline processes over 17,880 real-world job postings, combining high-dimensional unstructured textual signals (company profiles, job descriptions, requirements, and benefits) with structured metadata flags (company logo presence, telecommuting flags, education levels) to flag suspicious listings with high precision.

88% Fraud F1-Score 95% Precision 17,880 Postings Analyzed TF-IDF & Metadata Union

The Challenge: Deceptive Text & Sparsity

  • Subtle Deception: Scammers craft realistic-looking job titles, descriptions, and salary offerings that closely mirror legitimate companies.
  • Extreme Sparsity & High Dimensionality: Raw text features span thousands of unique vocabulary tokens, many of which appear infrequently or with minor typos.
  • Low Prior Probability: Only ~4.4% of total postings are fraudulent, meaning models must avoid flagging real company postings while capturing elusive scam patterns.

Methodology: Google PACE Framework

  1. Plan: Formulated the NLP objective to maximize the harmonic mean of precision and recall (F1-score) on the fraudulent class, ensuring recruitment platforms don't alienate legitimate recruiters.
  2. Analyze: Discovered that scam listings frequently lack company logos, exhibit generic company descriptions, promise unrealistic remote-work compensation, or omit specific skill requirements.
  3. Construct:
    • Built a comprehensive text preprocessing pipeline: HTML tag cleaning, lowercasing, punctuation stripping, tokenization, stop-word removal, and WordNet lemmatization.
    • Extracted TF-IDF n-grams (unigrams and bigrams) with sublinear term-frequency scaling.
    • Created a FeatureUnion marrying sparse TF-IDF text features with dense binary metadata flags (has_company_logo, has_questions, telecommuting).
    • Benchmarked Multinomial Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression (L2), Random Forest, and LightGBM with balanced class weights.
  4. Execute: Evaluated threshold calibration, achieving an optimal trade-off of 95% Precision and 88% F1-score on unseen holdout test sets.

Key Verified Results

  • 88% F1-Score on Fraudulent Class: Superior detection capability on rare scam postings.
  • 95% Precision: Ensures that 19 out of 20 flagged listings are genuine scams, dramatically minimizing false recruiter bans.
  • Feature Insights: Extracted dominant keywords strongly correlated with fraudulent postings (e.g., wire transfer, data entry clerk without experience, immediate hiring without interview).

Python, NLP (NLTK), TF-IDF Vectorization, Scikit-learn, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Pandas, Seaborn.
